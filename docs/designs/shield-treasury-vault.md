# Design: Shield — a treasury vault that gates the reload, not the trade

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-04

Mode: Builder (ETHGlobal hackathon)

## Problem Statement

Alex is 19, has $10k, and trades on Axiom — a non-custodial, high-speed Solana
execution wallet he holds the recovery phrase to. Shield does not, and cannot,
stop him from making a bad trade inside Axiom; Shield can still *observe*
what happens there onchain (that's the whole basis of the Graph layer below),
but it loses *control/enforcement* the moment funds land there, because
Axiom's non-custodial design means Shield never had authority over it in
principle (corrected wording — an earlier draft said "visibility is gone,"
which is wrong and contradicts the rest of this doc).

The actual loss pattern isn't "one bad trade." It's: lose the Axiom bankroll,
then — still angry, still down, in the same five minutes — pull more capital
out of the treasury and refill Axiom to chase it back. That refill is the one
moment Shield can observe (it's an onchain transfer out of a vault Shield
governs) and the one moment "calm Alex" would actually want gated. The hero
problem is not "stop every bad trade." It's **stop the emotionally-driven
reload after the bankroll has just been lost.**

## What Makes This Cool

No wallet today distinguishes "you're topping up because you have a plan" from
"you're topping up because you just got wrecked and want it back." Shield
does, because it's the only product that (a) actually reconstructs a user's
real realized-loss history from onchain data instead of trusting a
self-report, and (b) makes the resulting rule something the user's own
five-minutes-from-now self can't talk their way around. The demo moment —
a real, on-chain-rejected top-up, with a legible reason, followed by watching
a "raise the limit" request get timestamped 24 hours out — is a genuinely new
interaction nobody has shipped for this exact failure mode.

## Constraints

- Alex's execution wallet (Axiom) is non-custodial and stays that way. Shield
  never asks for its keys and never pretends to control it. It is a
  **registered, observed wallet**, not a controlled one.
- The Shield vault (the treasury) is the only account Shield has real
  authority over, and even there, authority is scoped to *delaying or
  blocking outbound transfers* — never seizing or redirecting funds.
- Basic fund recovery (get your money out) must work even if every off-chain
  service Shield operates is down. No external liveness dependency may sit
  between Alex and his own funds.
- Hackathon timebox: ~48-72 hours, one person, three unfamiliar SDKs
  (Substreams-for-Solana, Anchor, Chainlink CRE Confidential Workflows).
  Ledger explicitly out of scope for v1.
- A thin demo client (CLI or minimal web dashboard) is a required fourth
  surface, not implied by the three sponsor components — someone has to
  render the scoreboard and show the queued-transaction timestamps a judge
  watches. Scope it minimally: read-only queries against the subgraph and
  the vault's onchain state, plus the three demo actions (top-up attempt,
  raise-limit attempt, remove-Shield attempt). No account system, no
  polish beyond legibility.

## Premises

1. Guarding the top-up rather than every downstream trade isn't a product
   preference, it's the only thing Shield is structurally capable of, because
   Axiom being non-custodial means Shield never had authority over individual
   trades to begin with — agreed, revised from "Axiom is invisible" (wrong)
   to "Axiom is observable but not enforceable."
2. A commitment only means something if impulsive-Alex can't instantly weaken
   it — agreed, and now made concrete: every path that reduces friction
   (loosening a rule, changing a delay, removing Shield entirely) routes
   through the *same* delayed mechanism. There is no side door.
3. Solana-native is not a compromise. **Killed the earlier premise that
   Solana forced a choice between "the user" and "the architecture."**
   Squads V4 already proves delay + spending-limit primitives are buildable
   as Solana smart-account logic, and Substreams already indexes Solana
   natively — both facts hold regardless of Axiom specifically.
4. Single-transaction thresholds are gameable by structuring (four $400
   top-ups instead of one $1,600). Rolling cumulative funding velocity is
   therefore first-class in the rule engine, not a secondary detector.
5. The commitment's credibility depends on Shield's own operator being unable
   to quietly override it under pressure (user pleading, or a scammer posing
   as support) — this is why the high-risk evaluation is pushed into a
   confidential, signed verdict path rather than a plain backend check. But
   that path strengthens the commitment; it must never be the only way out.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Guard Vault only (minimal viable)
Single Anchor program with hardcoded thresholds, Substreams baseline computed
offline before the demo, CRE evaluates the top-up verdict. Rejected as the
primary approach — real but underselling the two hardest, most differentiated
pieces (live behavioral derivation, confidential evaluation) by pre-baking them.

### Approach B: Full constitution engine (ideal architecture)
Guard vault + full on-chain behavioral baseline computed inside Substreams +
full multi-rule CRE evaluation + Ledger Key Ring-encrypted constitution.
Rejected for v1: three unfamiliar SDKs all load-bearing simultaneously in a
48-72 hour window is the highest-variance option available, and a Ledger
integration bolted on before the core loop works risks diluting all three
sponsor stories instead of nailing two.

### Approach C+: Verdict-first, vault-second (chosen, corrected)
Split responsibility so the vault stays deliberately simple and *never*
depends on anything external for safety, while the interesting reasoning
(behavioral derivation, confidential risk evaluation) lives in Graph and CRE
and only ever *tightens* what the vault would otherwise allow. Corrected from
the original C per this session: the vault owns its own liveness/safety
invariants outright, rather than deferring "what's allowed right now" to a
verdict service.

## Recommended Approach

Three components, three sponsors, one non-negotiable rule: **the vault must
work correctly with every off-chain service dead.**

### 1. Shield Vault (Anchor program, Solana) — the safety floor

A PDA-owned treasury vault Alex deposits into, holding **one canonical USDC
token account only** (the pinned mint + pinned SPL Token program — native
SOL, other SPL mints, Token-2022 extensions, and any other token program are
explicitly rejected at the instruction level for v1). Deposits (inbound) are
always unrestricted — every invariant below governs outbound transfers only.

**Fixed movement surface (closes a real "arbitrary CPI" hole found in
review):** the vault exposes exactly one transfer primitive — vault USDC
account → pinned SPL Token program → a token account verified to be owned by
a **registered owner pubkey**. There is no generic execute/CPI passthrough,
no delegate-approval instruction, no account-closing instruction reachable
by anything but the program's own logic. Registration binds an **owner
pubkey** (not a token-account address) — this closes a real hole where Alex
could otherwise create a second USDC token account for the same wallet and
route around the classification; at transfer time the vault either derives
the canonical ATA for the registered owner or explicitly checks the supplied
token account's owner field against the registry.

**The deterministic discriminator:** every outbound transfer must target a
**registered owner pubkey** — there is no "unregistered destination" case,
the vault rejects transfers to anywhere else outright. Every registered
owner carries a stored **type tag**, **permanent and set exactly once, at
first registration** (no retagging in v1 — the immutability itself is what
makes "a cold tag can never apply to an owner with execution history" true
by construction): `execution` (a trading venue, e.g. Axiom) or `cold` (a
verified personal exit address).

**CRITICAL fix from review: `cold` is not economically cold.** The original
design treated any registered `cold` address as an unrestricted personal
wallet eligible for an instant, uncapped withdrawal — but once funds land in
an ordinary wallet Alex fully controls, Shield has no further authority, so
Alex could pre-register a `cold` address in a calm moment (paying the normal
24h registration delay once) and then, months later mid-loss-chase, drain
the **entire vault** to it instantly and forward it to Axiom himself. That
would make the 7-day full-exit row meaningless (effective exit time = 24h)
and defeats the whole product. Fixed by capping what "instant to cold" can
ever move: a `cold` transfer is instant **only below a small fixed emergency
cap** (default: a modest flat amount, e.g. low hundreds of dollars — tighter
than the top-up threshold, tightenable/loosenable like any other limit), and
**consumes the same global rolling-velocity accumulator as top-ups** (see
below) — so it cannot be used to bleed the vault out in capped increments
either. Any `cold` transfer above the cap is not instant; it is not a
separate row at all, it routes through the full 7-day exit (row 5) with the
amount and destination locked into that proposal. There is no medium-speed
path to move a large balance to an arbitrary personal wallet — that gap is
now closed rather than papered over.

| # | Action | Delay | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tighten** — lower any allowance, lengthen any cooldown, add a restriction | Instant | Never gated — impulsive-Alex can always make the vault *stricter*. Monotonicity is now explicit (a review-found bug: an earlier draft listed "shorten a delay" under this row, which is a weakening, not a tightening) |
| 2 | **Loosen** — raise any allowance, **shorten** any cooldown, remove a restriction | 24h | Queued, cancelable by Alex any time before it executes |
| 3 | Change the delay parameters themselves, in the loosening direction | 24h | Same path as loosening — no separate faster route; a change that only lengthens a delay is row 1 (instant) |
| 4 | **Top-up** — any transfer to an `execution`-tagged owner | 30m, CRE-extendable | The row the hero demo exercises. Includes the static on-chain rolling-velocity check (below). **When an active behavioral cooldown is set (see below), row 4 applies to every transfer to an execution-tagged owner, including ones that would otherwise be under any per-transaction floor** — closes a review-found gap where a user mid-loss-streak could still send meaningful amounts as "micro-transfers" |
| 5 | **Full Shield exit** — uninstall the vault, or any `cold` transfer above the emergency cap; destination and amount are locked into the proposal at creation time, not substitutable at execution | 7d | The longest delay in the system, on purpose — this is the one impulsive-Alex is most likely to reach for mid-tilt |
| 6 | **Capped emergency de-risk** — transfer to a `cold`-tagged owner, at or below the fixed emergency cap only | Instant, always, including with CRE and Graph both dead | Shares the vault-global rolling-velocity accumulator with row 4 — cannot be used to structure around the cap via repeated small transfers |

**Registration asymmetry, as a principle:** registering a brand-new owner as
`execution` is **instant** (row 1) — it can only ever subject that owner to
*more* scrutiny. Registering a brand-new owner as `cold` is **always the
24h delayed path** (row 2/3) — `cold` status is what grants any withdrawal
privilege at all, so it's exactly the action a scammer's "send it to this
recovery address" message would target. **Removing** a registration (either
type) is a **tighten, instant** — it can only reduce what the vault will do,
never expand it; the underlying owner's historical type tag is retained
permanently even after removal, so it can never be re-added under a
different type later.

**Static anti-structuring floor, now vault-global and atomic:** the vault
stores one compact rolling sum (timestamped running total, 24h window,
`rolling_before + proposed_amount >= threshold` is the exact comparison used
everywhere) across **all** outbound transfers to `execution`-tagged owners
**and** capped `cold` transfers together — not per-destination, so splitting
a reload across multiple registered execution wallets, or between an
execution wallet and a cold wallet, cannot create extra allowance. The check
and the state update happen atomically with the transfer, in the same
instruction. Graph/CRE layer smarter, personalized, history-aware thresholds
on top of this (including setting the active behavioral-cooldown flag
referenced in row 4); the basic structuring protection holds even if Graph
and CRE are both completely dead.

**Program upgrade authority (CRITICAL fix from review):** if Shield retains
Solana program-upgrade authority, Shield itself can redeploy logic that
ignores every cooldown — directly contradicting the premise that not even
Shield's own operator can cave. **The deployed program's upgrade authority
must be finalized (set to `None`, immutable) before the judged deployment.**
This is a deploy-time step, not a runtime feature, and it's now a first-class
invariant (see Invariants section) rather than an implicit assumption.

**Proposal identity and lifecycle, fully specified (review found this was
underspecified):** every proposal is bound, at creation, to a monotonic
nonce/ID and an action hash covering vault, category, action type,
destination owner, amount/parameters, created-at, execute-after, expiry, and
the configuration version in effect at creation. Execution is one-shot
(consumes the proposal). **Three independent proposal categories**, one
pending slot each: *rule-change* (rows 1-3), *top-up* (row 4), *full-exit*
(row 5 — which also covers any above-cap `cold` transfer, since that's
explicitly routed through the same 7-day path and shares its pending slot,
not a separate one). **Row 6 (at-or-below-cap `cold` transfer) never queues
at all** — it's instant by definition, so it never occupies a proposal slot
and can never be silently overwritten by, or overwrite, a pending full-exit.
A new proposal in the same category replaces and restarts the cooldown on
the prior one; different categories queue and execute independently.
**Stale-proposal semantics:** if a
later tighten intervenes on a pending loosen, the pending loosen does not
inherit extra authority from the tighten — grandfathering only preserves the
waiting period already accrued on a proposal that was valid when created, it
never grants a stale proposal power it wouldn't have if re-proposed today.
**Cold start:** a vault with no funding history gets a fixed conservative
default rule set (small top-up threshold, no active behavioral cooldown)
until the Graph layer has enough history for the Guardian to recommend real
numbers.

The vault has one more entry point: **accept a CRE verdict, bound to the
exact vault + proposal nonce/hash + program ID + expiry, that either extends
a proposed row-4 cooldown to a pre-agreed longer bound or confirms it
proceeds on schedule** — a strict binary, no third state, never reusable
against a different proposal, never able to shorten the floor or redirect
funds (see CRE section below). Critically, the vault's own table above is
the *floor*: CRE can make things more cautious, never less, and if CRE is
unreachable the vault falls back to its own static rules — including the
static velocity check and any already-set behavioral-cooldown flag — rather
than blocking. CRE strengthens the commitment; it never becomes a liveness
dependency for getting safely out.

**PDA and account integrity:** vault state, the vault's USDC token account,
the destination registry, and every proposal account use deterministic
seeds plus explicit owner/mint/authority checks on every instruction — a
caller cannot substitute a lookalike account for any of these.

**Explicitly out of scope for v1:** loss of the vault-authority key itself
(no social-recovery/guardian scheme — punted, same as any single-key wallet
today; stated prominently as an accepted limitation, not hidden). Retagging
an owner after registration. Multi-asset support (USDC only; native SOL,
other SPL mints, and Token-2022 are explicitly rejected, not silently
mishandled). **Rolling-velocity state-management fallback:** the 24h pruned
rolling sum is, like FIFO P&L, novel Anchor account-state work and deserves
the same explicit fallback — if a fully pruned sliding window proves too
complex in the time budget, fall back to a fixed set of six 4-hour buckets
(sum-and-rotate, not prune-on-read), which is strictly simpler and still
closes the structuring gap.

**Recovery does not depend on Shield existing:** if the Shield frontend,
backend, and every off-chain service disappear entirely, Alex must still be
able to submit `cancel`, execute a matured proposal, and drive the full exit
path directly against Solana RPC using an open-source, minimal CLI client —
no Shield-operated infrastructure in the loop for any of it. This is a
Success Criterion (below), not just an aspiration.

### 2. The Graph (Substreams-for-Solana) — the behavioral memory

A real Substreams module, not a wrapped API call, tracks two kinds of
accounts per user: the Shield vault, and one or more **registered execution
wallets** (`execution`-tagged addresses from the vault's own registry above
— Axiom, or any wallet Alex explicitly tells Shield to watch). Registration
is itself a signed onchain action from the vault authority — adding a watch
target is not a passive/implicit thing Shield infers. It parses raw
instruction data for the relevant programs (Axiom's deposit flow,
Jupiter/Raydium/pump.fun swaps in the execution wallet) and derives, as a
queryable subgraph, **every metric below aggregated per-user across all of
that user's registered execution wallets, not per-wallet** — otherwise
splitting a reload across two registered wallets would reopen the exact
structuring gap Premise 4 closes for a single wallet:

- Top-up totals and return totals, per execution wallet ("$4,100 sent, $2,810
  came back")
- Realized P&L via cost-basis (FIFO) on the execution wallet's trade history
  — flagged as the single riskiest piece of engineering in this plan (see
  Open Questions); the fallback if FIFO proves too heavy in the time budget
  is **closed-cycle USDC accounting** (total USDC sent to the execution
  wallet minus total USDC returned from it, counting only completed
  round-trips) — corrected from an earlier "net-flow" fallback that
  subtracted "current holdings value," which silently required a price
  oracle to value open non-USDC positions and would have added an
  unbudgeted fourth SDK dependency. Closed-cycle accounting needs no
  oracle, is strictly simpler than FIFO, and still supports every demo
  claim in Success Criteria (it just doesn't count unrealized P&L on
  positions still open, which the demo doesn't need)
- Loss-streak sequences: consecutive realized losses, using a fixed
  **7-day recency window** (a loss older than 7 days doesn't count toward
  the current streak)
- **Median funding size** (the one baseline metric used everywhere in this
  doc — top-up size is compared against this, not a separate "median trade"
  figure) over a fixed **30-day rolling window**
- **Rolling cumulative funding velocity** — sum of transfers from vault to
  execution wallets inside a fixed **24-hour sliding window**, so four $400
  top-ups inside that window read as one $1,600 event — first-class, not
  bolted onto the single-transaction check

The "Guardian" is not a fourth component — it's a bounded, hardcoded
heuristic layer on top of these five Substreams outputs, not an open-ended
recommendation engine, and it runs **once, at setup/re-setup time, not per
top-up** (that live per-event evaluation is CRE's job, described next — the
two must not be confused, since they read similar-looking numbers for
different purposes). Example: "given this wallet's history shows loss-streak
≥ 2 events historically correlating with reload sizes averaging 9x median,
propose a static row-4 top-up hold of 6h **and arm the behavioral-cooldown
flag for the next 6h**" — a one-time threshold-setting recommendation Alex
accepts or edits, which becomes both the row-4 cooldown parameter and the
trigger condition for the flag that (per the vault section) makes row 4
apply to *every* execution transfer, not just above-threshold ones, while
armed. It reads Graph data and writes an initial (or updated, via the normal
tighten/loosen paths) vault rule set; it has no standing authority to change
vault state itself.

### 3. Chainlink CRE Confidential Workflows — the tamper-resistant judgment call

A CRE workflow with a TEE handler evaluates the harder, more subjective
question a static threshold can't: does this specific top-up, in this
specific context, look like a loss-chasing reload? **Correction from
review:** an earlier draft claimed Alex's "exact stored rule thresholds"
were the confidential input — that's wrong, because any threshold the vault
itself enforces (top-up %, cooldown length, emergency cap) necessarily lives
in Anchor account state, which is public onchain data by construction; it
cannot simultaneously be private. What's actually confidential is narrower
and more honest: the CRE workflow's **evaluation logic itself** — the
specific heuristic/weighting that decides whether a given combination of
public signals (loss streak, velocity, top-up size relative to baseline)
crosses the line into "extend the cooldown" — plus any additional
behavioral signal CRE incorporates that Shield chooses not to publish to
the subgraph at all. Keeping the *decision process* private (not the
already-public numbers it reads) is what stops an attacker from
reverse-engineering the exact boundary of a bypass, and stops Shield's own
operator from being pressured into quietly tuning one user's evaluation,
without overclaiming that public account state is somehow secret.

The verdict is binary and maps to one concrete vault effect: **extend** the
proposed cooldown to a pre-agreed longer bound (this specific top-up looks
like loss-chasing), or **confirm** it proceeds on the schedule the vault's
own static table already set (no reason to treat it differently). There is
no third "flag for review" state — anything CRE isn't confident enough to
extend, it confirms, and the vault's static default stands. **Verdict
binding (review found this underspecified):** a valid verdict is bound to
the exact vault address, the proposal's nonce/hash, the program ID, and an
expiry; it is never reusable against a different proposal, can only extend
(never shorten) the floor, and can never redirect funds or alter a
destination.

The structural reason this needs to be confidential rather than a plain
backend check: the commitment only means something if Shield's own operator
can't quietly weaken one user's evaluation under pressure — a user begging
mid-tilt, or a scammer posing as Shield support. A TEE + DON-attested verdict
makes the "calm you" promise credible against Shield itself, not just against
Alex's own impulses. This is additive, not a dependency: the vault's own
static table already provides the safety floor, so a dead or compromised CRE
workflow degrades Shield to "static rules only," never to "no protection" and
never to "can't get your money out." **Verdict delivery to Solana** (signing
key, onchain verification of the DON's signature, relayer vs. direct
submission) is unresolved — see Open Questions; this is the second
demo-blocking unknown alongside Axiom's address pattern, not a minor
implementation detail.

### What's explicitly cut for v1

- Ledger — no integration in the initial architecture. Revisit only after
  Graph + Anchor vault + CRE are independently working end-to-end. If it
  comes back, the only version that survived earlier adversarial review is
  Key Ring encrypting the stored rule parameters themselves (hardware as root
  of trust for commitment integrity), never hardware-tap-to-override.
- Separate detectors for "concentration spike" and "unseen recipient" — both
  fold into the same velocity/anomaly primitive the top-up guard already
  needs, rather than becoming their own code paths.
- Any multi-chain surface. Solana only.

## Open Questions

Ranked by how demo-blocking they are, not by discovery order. The first two
must be resolved before any other work starts — everything else in the plan
assumes an answer to both.

1. **Axiom's real deposit-address pattern** on Solana mainnet (or a realistic
   devnet stand-in). If Axiom uses ephemeral/per-session deposit addresses
   rather than a stable one, the "registered execution wallet" model breaks
   and needs a different detection strategy (e.g., watching Alex's own
   outbound transfers rather than a fixed destination) before anything else
   is built.
2. **CRE verdict delivery to Solana** — what key signs it, how a Solana
   program verifies a DON-attested signature (custom Ed25519 check vs. a
   relayer), and whether CRE targets Solana natively today. Validate a
   minimal CRE CLI simulation on day 1, with a hard kill deadline: **if there
   is no working Solana signature-verification path by end of day 1, switch
   to the plain signed backend check permanently and don't revisit it** —
   same discipline as the P&L fallback below, not an open-ended "if too
   fragile." This keeps the vault's safety invariants intact either way, but
   is acknowledged as weakening the operator-tamper-resistance claim in
   Premise 5, not a costless degradation.
3. **Global cut order if time runs out**, stated explicitly rather than left
   implicit in Next Steps' ordering: the vault (with its static floor,
   including the anti-structuring check) is never cut — it's the one
   component the whole product's safety claim depends on. If forced to ship
   only two of the three sponsor stories, cut CRE before Graph: a
   "Graph-derived scoreboard + statically-gated vault, no confidential
   verdict layer" is still a complete, demoable product; a vault with a
   confidential verdict layer but no real behavioral history is not.
4. **Substreams FIFO P&L is the single riskiest engineering item in the
   plan** — correct cost-basis accounting across multi-hop Jupiter routes,
   Raydium, and pump.fun, in an SDK never used before, is a bigger lift than
   either the vault or the CRE workflow. If it's not tracking by the halfway
   point of the timebox, fall back to closed-cycle USDC accounting (see
   Graph section) rather than losing the whole scoreboard.
5. Exact bounds for how much CRE's verdict is allowed to move the vault's
   static cooldown (row 4's default threshold, the extend-vs-confirm cutoff)
   — needs to be pinned down before the Anchor program is written, since
   it's part of the program's own validation logic, not just the CRE
   workflow's.
6. Whether the Guardian's heuristic formula (loss-streak/velocity → proposed
   rule) is computed inside the Substreams module itself or downstream in
   the demo client — downstream is lower-risk and is the current intent for
   v1; pushing it into Substreams is a stretch goal, not a requirement.

## Success Criteria

- A real wallet (not fabricated data) has its top-up/return totals, realized
  P&L, loss streaks, and rolling funding velocity correctly derived by the
  Substreams pipeline, aggregated per-user across all registered execution
  wallets, and queryable as a subgraph.
- The Anchor vault correctly enforces all six rows of the invariant table
  above, independent of whether CRE is reachable — demonstrable by killing
  the CRE service mid-demo and showing the capped emergency de-risk transfer
  still works instantly (and a below-cap amount only), and by showing the
  static on-chain rolling-velocity check still blocks a structured top-up
  (four small transfers) with CRE dead.
- The primary demo sequence executes against real on-chain state: scoreboard
  reveal → live blocked top-up (9x baseline, violates loss-chasing rule) →
  attempted limit raise queued 24h → attempted full removal queued 7d — with
  a judge able to verify each state change on a block explorer.
- The secondary scam-address case: Alex, after a fake support message, tries
  to send a large amount to a brand-new address. Because that address has
  never been `cold`-tagged, it cannot receive any transfer at all — the
  earliest it could be added as a trusted exit is 24h out via the same
  delayed registration path as any other rule loosening, and even once
  registered, only a small emergency-capped amount is instant — giving Alex
  a cooling-off window (and a hard cap on damage) the scam depends on him
  not having.
- With the Shield frontend, backend, and every other off-chain service
  killed, a matured proposal can still be executed and the emergency de-risk
  transfer can still be sent, using only the open-source recovery CLI
  against Solana RPC directly — demonstrable live in the demo as the "you
  don't need to trust us to stay online" beat.
- The deployed program's upgrade authority is verifiably `None` (immutable)
  on the judged deployment — checkable by any judge against the onchain
  program account, not just asserted in the README.

## Distribution Plan

Hackathon submission: public repo with README documenting the Substreams
module, the Anchor program, and the CRE workflow, plus a 2-4 minute demo
video showing the primary sequence against real on-chain state (required for
The Graph track). No packaged distribution beyond the repo and a devnet/
testnet-deployed program address for judges to inspect directly.

## Next Steps

1. Resolve Open Questions #1 and #2 first (Axiom's address pattern, CRE's
   Solana verdict-delivery mechanism) — both block downstream work and both
   need same-day answers, in parallel if possible, before committing further
   plan time.
2. Stand up the Substreams module against a real wallet with real loss
   history; get top-up/return totals, loss streaks, median size, and rolling
   velocity queryable first, P&L last (it's the riskiest sub-piece — start
   with closed-cycle USDC accounting, upgrade to FIFO only if time allows).
3. Write the Anchor vault: single propose→cooldown→execute-or-cancel state
   machine, six rows/three proposal categories per the invariant table,
   owner-pubkey-based registration, fixed single-instruction transfer
   surface (no arbitrary CPI), capped emergency de-risk sharing the same
   velocity accumulator as top-ups. Get this fully working and demoable
   *before* CRE is wired in — it must stand alone.
4. Build the open-source recovery CLI (cancel / execute-matured / full-exit
   against Solana RPC directly) alongside the vault, not as an afterthought
   — it's what makes "recovery doesn't depend on Shield existing" a
   demonstrable claim rather than an assertion.
5. Build the thin demo client against the vault + subgraph — even before CRE
   is wired in, the primary demo sequence (scoreboard → blocked top-up →
   queued limit-raise → queued full-exit) should be fully walkable on static
   rules alone.
6. Validate a minimal CRE Confidential Workflow simulation (should already be
   answered by step 1); wire the bounded extend/confirm verdict → vault
   cooldown-adjustment path once the vault and client are stable.
7. **Finalize (burn) the program's upgrade authority before the judged
   deployment.** This is a one-command deploy step, easy to forget under

## Invariants

The small set that must never be violated, regardless of implementation
detail — anything in this doc can change; these cannot, or the product
stops meaning what it claims to mean:

1. **No instant unrestricted escape.** No path moves funds instantly from
   the vault into an account from which the user can immediately reach an
   execution venue with a materially large amount.
2. **Every weakening operation is delayed.** Higher limits, shorter delays,
   removal of a restriction, addition of a new escape destination,
   disabling/closing Shield, and changing authorities or program
   upgradeability must never have a faster side path than the slowest
   equivalent action already in this doc.
3. **Tightening is genuinely monotonic.** An instant "tighten" action can
   only leave Alex equally or more protected — never less.
4. **Only the Shield program can move vault funds, through one narrowly
   defined transfer path.** No arbitrary CPI, delegate approval, authority
   replacement, or user-supplied signing path.
5. **Destination classification cannot be evaded through account
   substitution.** The immutable type belongs to the registered owner
   pubkey, checked against the pinned asset/mint, not to a token-account
   address the owner can multiply.
6. **Execution funding is globally aggregated.** Splitting a reload across
   transactions, registered execution wallets, or the emergency-cap path
   cannot create additional allowance beyond the single rolling accumulator.
7. **An active behavioral cooldown applies to every execution reload.** A
   large transfer cannot be laundered into permitted micro-transfers while
   a cooldown is armed.
8. **CRE can only make Shield stricter.** A missing, forged, replayed, or
   malicious verdict can never shorten the onchain safety floor or redirect
   funds.
9. **Proposals are immutable, versioned, one-shot, and replay-safe.** What
   matures and executes is exactly what was proposed, nothing substituted.
10. **Recovery does not depend on Shield existing.** With the legitimate
    authority key and enough time, Alex has an entirely onchain path — via
    the open-source recovery CLI against Solana RPC directly — to recover
    his assets even if Graph, CRE, the frontend, and Shield the company all
    disappear.
11. **The deployed enforcement code cannot be silently replaced.** Program
    upgrade authority is finalized (immutable) before the judged
    deployment — otherwise every invariant above is cosmetic, since Shield
    could redeploy around any of them.

## Accepted Limitations

Real, but deliberate — not bugs, and stated prominently rather than left
implicit:

- Shield only protects assets voluntarily placed inside the Shield vault.
  Alex can always use a different wallet, a CEX, fresh external capital, or
  funds already sitting inside Axiom — Shield was never going to be able to
  prevent that, and doesn't claim to.
- Once funds reach a registered execution wallet, Shield can observe onchain
  activity there but cannot enforce individual trades (this is Premise 1,
  restated as an accepted limitation rather than a caveat).
- A determined user who waits through the legitimate weakening or full-exit
  period can always leave. That's intentional — Shield protects against
  short-lived impulses, not a multi-day, fully deliberate decision.
- Loss of the vault-authority key or device has no recovery path in v1 —
  the normal single-key self-custody failure mode, stated here rather than
  buried in an "out of scope" footnote.
- Graph or CRE downtime degrades personalized behavioral protection down to
  the deterministic onchain floor. It must never compromise custody or the
  recovery path.
- Minor Solana `Clock` timestamp variance is acceptable for 30-minute,
  24-hour, and 7-day windows — this doc never promises second-perfect
  timing.
- v1 supports canonical USDC only. Native SOL, Token-2022, and multi-asset
  valuation are unsupported, not silently mishandled.
- Behavior across unregistered wallets, CEXs, and off-chain/unrelated
  payment rails cannot be reconstructed by the Graph layer — Shield's
  picture of Alex's financial life is bounded by what he's registered.

This doc went through 3 rounds of independent Claude-subagent adversarial
