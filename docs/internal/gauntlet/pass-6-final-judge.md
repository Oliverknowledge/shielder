# Pass 6 — final judge

One judge, told the deadline was two hours away and to be ungenerous.

## Scores

Technicality 8 · Originality 8 · Practicality 6 · Usability 7 · WOW 6.

The reasoning that matters: technicality is marked for integration breadth
rather than a hard problem, and marked *down* because "the headline mechanism
(rolling 24h limit) provably doesn't hold". Practicality is capped by testnet,
no users, and the $30.50 already stranded in the deployed contract. WOW is
capped because there is no video, no public repo and no hosted URL, so "the
impression is competence and integrity rather than spectacle".

## What it said distinguishes this from the median

- It clears the *anti*-qualification clauses on purpose. The Graph's criteria say
  querying one subgraph does not qualify; most entries will do exactly that.
- The CRE integration has a **negative control** — a run with the secret broken,
  showing the workflow die before any HTTP call. "Almost nobody does this. It
  converts 'a secret is present' into 'the secret is load-bearing', which is the
  actual clause."
- A verdict signed in the enclave changed state on a deployed immutable contract.
- Calibrated honesty: declining tracks with reasons, pinning three of its own
  contract defects as tests, stating the Substreams zero-row limitation in the
  app itself.

What does **not** distinguish it: technical difficulty, traction, demo readiness.

## The one thing it would have thrown the submission out for

The docs described the CRE transcript as showing `realisedLoss24h=3500000 …
triggered=true`, and the committed transcript — regenerated by this gauntlet five
minutes after those docs were last edited — showed zero and false, with a
different explanation. On the track whose criterion is *"provide evidence of
successful simulation"*, the paragraph pointing at the evidence was wrong.

Fixed. It is the clearest illustration of the second cycle's warning: the repo
was changing faster than its own evidence.

## Also fixed from this pass

- The daily limit's disclosed defects were in the threat model and not in the
  app, which is exactly the asymmetry the project claims not to have.
- Home and Activity narrated an old verdict as "$69.50 realised" while the
  assessment on the same screen said $0. "Attested" now.
- `/api/health` announced `demo: true` on the submitted testnet deployment,
  because the guard only caught mainnet — in the payload the README tells a judge
  to curl.
- The evidence transcript's header timestamp disagreed with its own log lines.

## Its answer on disclosing the contract defects

Asked straight whether publishing three defects in your own deployed contract
helps or hurts in a judged competition:

> Keep it. It is a strength. A sponsor judge is not auditing for exploitability;
> they are calibrating whether the rest of your claims can be trusted, and
> `KnownDefects.t.sol` — tests that assert the *wrong* behaviour on purpose — is
> the single most credible artefact in this repo.

With one correction, now applied: it was too prominent in the submission's own
"what the contract enforces" section, and it was absent from the app. Disclosing
in docs while the UI still promised a hard limit was the actual liability.

## Its closing instruction

> Items 1 and 2 are worth more than 3–8 combined. If time collapses, ship the
> repo public, spend fifteen minutes on the CRE paragraph, and put every
> remaining minute into the video.

The CRE paragraph is done. The other two are human actions.
